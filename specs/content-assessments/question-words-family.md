## Topic, level, and scope

2026-10-28. The user approved seven related packs covering information questions,
their answers, negative questions and negative replies, in the established lesson
style. All new packs and lessons are classified `0 - A1.3`. This is controlled

## Sources

- [Aalto: Questions](https://openlearning.aalto.fi/mod/book/view.php?chapterid=406&id=7549):
  information-question order and location/direction interrogatives. Its simplified
  olla/other-verbs distinction is insufficient as a universal mikä/mitä rule.
- [A Taste of Finnish: grammar](https://tasteoffinnish.fi/grammar/): countable and
  substance identification, partitive descriptions, person forms, location cases
  and origin. Apply bounded examples rather than a blanket exact/inexact rule.
- [Kielitoimisto: question mark](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/kysymysmerkki/):
  ordinary information questions versus -ko/-kö questions.
- [Kielitoimisto: pronominal -inen combinations](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/yhdyssana-vai-ei-tamantyyppinen-vai-taman-tyyppinen/):
  joined/separate spelling alternatives and minkälainen.
- [Kielitoimisto: kuka and ketä](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/pronominit-kuka-keta/):
  human interrogative and negative-pronoun forms; bounded kukaan subject replies.
- [Kielitoimisto: the negative verb](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/konjunktiot-eika-ei-eika-ja-ei/):
  negative person forms and the distinction from the standalone answer particle.
- [Kielikello: koska and sillä](https://kielikello.fi/koska-ja-silla/):
  reason clauses; this family uses milloin for unambiguous time questions.
- [Kielitoimisto: numbers and grouping](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/luvut-ja-numerot-numeroiden-ryhmittely/):
  write the supplied whole-number expressions as single Finnish number words.

answering assess the same bounded relationship; the supporting person/verb forms
are taught in the explanation and supplied when their formation is not assessed.
Each pack has a local prerequisite chain; vocabulary provenance does not assume
that an earlier external pack makes a word available to the current reference.

### Counts, demands and Review boundary

Planned: 35 lessons, 35 Focused tests, seven final Reviews, **840 scored questions**
(20 Focused questions and four Review questions per target), and **105 optional
practice questions** (three per lesson). Maximum pack size: 192 scored questions.
The common Focused minimum is justified by eight distinct dialogue situations per
target: recognition, question construction, answer construction, interpretation
and misconception repair supply different retrieval demands. Ten situations per
target support scored work; two further situations support optional practice.
No cosmetic prompt wrappers count as new coverage. Review questions retrieve
previously taught relationships in interleaved order and introduce no new skill.
Reciprocal mastery partners stay within one target, response demand and comparable
difficulty, with different Finnish surfaces and intervening retrieval.

### Prerequisites, vocabulary and predicted misconceptions

Existing olla, demonstrative, partitive and genitive packs provide background, but
every new pack is locally complete for its assessed decisions. Supply supporting
verb forms, finite forms, number words and non-target stems before submission.
New versus reused lexical items follow the transitive local lesson chain, at most
ten new scored words per Focused lesson. Transparent combinations remain separate
words; grammar interrogatives and genuinely taught answer elements stay in grammar
teaching. Inventory question recall independently from the visible task, excluding
translated supplied forms and word-order tokens, including supporting words in
Finnish-to-English tasks.

Teach countable identification versus substance identification, mitkä selection
versus mitä classification, human singular/plural reference, question/answer person
changes, at/from/to case relationships, weekday -na/-nä, clock kello, descriptive
number/mass agreement, nationality versus residence, and negative verb agreement.
Predict errors from English question inversion and do-support, added -ko/-kö in an
ordinary information question, wrong direction, bare dictionary-form answers,
adjective agreement, conjugating the main verb after a negative verb, and treating
a negative information question as a yes/no question. Correcting an assumption is
a distinct response skill: a negative question may have an affirmative answer.
Do not force unnatural negative versions of every isolated question word.

### Lesson responsibilities and progression

Objectives state the outcome; Meaning and pattern explains the decision; Answering
the question gives actual full/short replies and the relevant formation; key points
summarize those sections. Vocabulary belongs only in the dedicated three lists.
Worked question and answer examples explain the actual words and construction.
Common mistakes teach predictable corrections. Three optional questions apply the
same target without scored state. Full Finnish-construction meanings are visible
before answering; Finnish-to-English questions assess those meanings instead.
Prompts remain at most 40 words and disambiguate second-person number. Natural
standard written alternatives are explicit, including pronoun omission when
appropriate, English hän alternatives, and supported joined spellings.

### Finnish, meanings and answering

The complete 35-target inventory was compared with the source-grounded blueprint,
including independently prepared singular/plural relationships and the separate
substance descriptions. Each lesson explicitly teaches answers, provides natural
short replies, and demonstrates full replies with word-specific construction
steps. The 28 positive lessons each include a fully supplied negative/corrective
comparison; the final seven lessons assess negative formation, nobody/nothing and
affirmative correction of a negative assumption. Negative miksi questions receive
actual reasons rather than bare negations. The requested meaning disambiguates
correction from confirmation.

The scope avoids an exact/inexact or olla/other-verbs universal rule for mikä/mitä.
Countable identification, selection, action objects and substances are separate
targets. Location/source/destination, weekday and clock patterns use bounded
taught forms. Kotoisin marks origin; nationality is distinguished from residence
or travel origin. Millainen covers general qualities, whereas minkä näköinen
requests visible appearance. Supporting genitive objects, finite verbs, plural
descriptions and number expressions are explicitly supplied when they are outside
the Focused decision. These are controlled present-tense examples.

Corrections during the audit included lowercase olen/olemme after koska, English
I capitalization, single-word Finnish numbers, genuine because-replies, and
contextual word roles. For example, a profession after olla identifies the person;
a substance after juoda is an object, while the same substance after olla gives
an identity. The first grammar section defines the terminology actually used in
the worked examples; exercise feedback also defines relevant terms before the
construction. The clock-time phrase explains mihin as at what rather than as a
destination in that context.
