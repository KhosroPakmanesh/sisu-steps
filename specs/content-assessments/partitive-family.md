## Verified sources and decisions

- [Yle Supisuomea: partitive](https://yle.fi/a/20-156324): common singular formation, quantities, substance amounts and introductory plural use. Its spoken video transcripts are not copied into the authored standard-written corpus; broad teaching shortcuts about all numbers or negative sentences are narrowed explicitly.
- [Osaan suomea: word types](https://www.osaansuomea.fi/osaan-sanatyypit/): regular and changing i/e word families. Word-family membership is taught for a bounded inventory, not inferred from spelling alone.
- [Kielitoimiston ohjepankki: object case](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/objekti-han-luki-kirjaa-kirjan-milloin-partitiivi-milloin-ei/): negation, unbounded quantity and open activity. Partitive does not assert that an activity will never finish; negative identity statements are not turned into partitives.
- [Osaan suomea: pronouns](https://www.osaansuomea.fi/pronominit/): standard personal object-pronoun forms; plural pronouns do not require productive plural noun morphology.
- [A Taste of Finnish: grammar](https://tasteoffinnish.fi/grammar/): standard case patterns, demonstratives and supporting verb constructions. Spoken sections are excluded.
- [University of Helsinki: describing substances](https://blogs.helsinki.fi/kielenoppijantyokalupakki/kopulalause-kahvi-on-kuumaa/): restricted singular substance predicates, distinguished from attributive agreement and countable-object descriptions.
- [Eila ja Ossi](https://kansalaisopistojenliitto.fi/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/eila_ja_ossi_ktol_2007.pdf): measurements and beginner usage contexts.
- [OPH A1.1–A1.3 descriptors](https://www.oph.fi/fi/koulutus-ja-tutkinnot/kirjallisen-kielitaidon-maamerkit-taitotasot-a11-a13): bounded everyday communication; the proposed grammar sequence is an authorial choice, not an official CEFR grammar checklist.

### Prerequisites and lexical map

The current validator resolves prerequisites inside each pack. The first lesson in each pack therefore introduces its target from first principles; later targets declare earlier locally taught skills. Supporting verbs, complete non-target case forms, locations and number words are visibly supplied whenever producing them is not assessed. No score silently depends on completing another pack. Morphology examples can be isolated forms because formation itself is their explicit target; usage examples are complete sentences.

- Forms: familiar concrete nouns (house, car, toy, ball, cup, pen), then bounded -ta, e, -nen, i→e, -si and consonant-stem inventories. Regular plural words reuse familiar nouns and add school, shelf, pillow and street. Each transformation displays base meaning and number-specific target meaning. Eight-word groups support recognition, production and repair; five-word exceptional groups need four distinct demands rather than additional rare vocabulary.
- Quantities: familiar countable nouns and food/drink substances; counting/measurement words support case choice. Reuse substance words across the measured/unmeasured targets. Contrast noun number with the number of referents, especially two cars versus many cars. No inflected numeral paradigm is assessed.
- Sentences: reusable food/drink, transport, home and reading vocabulary. Each usage target introduces at most ten recall words; recurring words keep their original English/type through local prerequisites. Ordinary positive and negative frames are supplied in Finnish production. Languages use singular case forms. The ongoing lesson avoids claiming completion or non-completion in the future.
- Adjectives: familiar size, colour and temperature descriptions. The case-choice task supplies complete adjective alternatives when plural stem formation is outside the target. Singular substance predicates receive their own preparation; full plural predicate rules remain deferred.
- Pronouns/questions: pronouns and question words are assessed grammar and belong in grammar sections rather than lexical lists. Supporting verbs/nouns are lexical words. Person, distance and prior-reference cues are visible; English second-person meanings explicitly identify number. Gender-neutral hän/häntä translations accept faithful he/she or him/her alternatives where applicable.

Final lexical entries and question recall arrays will be independently checked against rendered text and aligned sentence parts. Transparent phrases are component words; no invented fixed-expression bundling is used to evade the ten-word limit. The dedicated vocabulary area owns lexical lists; contextual glosses and morphological tables remain in teaching.

### Misconceptions, variation and mastery

Predictable errors include dropping the original vowel, choosing the wrong harmony, copying a weak consonant stem, applying one i-word rule everywhere, using a plural noun after kaksi, treating all food words as invariably partitive, changing every negative-sentence noun, treating a process as necessarily never completed, leaving an adjective uninflected and using a subject pronoun as a partitive object.

Recognition precedes controlled production and interpretation/repair. Twenty-four items are justified by eight distinct lexical/context cases and three different response demands; twenty-item targets have smaller or more tightly bounded contrasts. These are coverage allocations rather than a pack-size target. Reviews add fresh contexts and forms of retrieval. Optional items are separate from scored tasks. Mutual mastery partners have the same target, comparable demand and different surface answers, separated in authored order. Morphology may use fewer interaction types; word-order tiles are not forced into a case-choice test when the tiles would supply the decision being assessed.
