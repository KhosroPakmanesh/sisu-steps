# Design-system principles

Sisu Steps should feel calm, encouraging, precise, and private during repeated Finnish study. Teaching clarity is more important than decorative novelty.

## Principles

- Keep study workflows readable and scannable at desktop and narrow mobile widths.
- Make lessons, scored tests, optional practice, skipped answers, corrections, and delayed mastery visibly distinct.
- Make destructive actions and local download/restore actions explicit.
- Communicate correctness, status, and risk with text or iconography in addition to color.
- Use consistent spacing, typography, borders, radius, focus, control, elevation, and breakpoint tokens.
- Reuse established components and interaction patterns before inventing a variant.
- Keep learner input and error messages visible and recoverable when an operation fails.

## Product feel

- An immersive Nordic school notebook: warm paper, softened ink, muted blue and pine, and restrained red-pencil accents.
- Quiet, low-glare, and uncluttered during long study sessions in both light and dark appearances.
- Beginner-friendly without looking childish.
- Trustworthy and focused on learning.
- Local and under the learner's control.
- Handwriting is decorative only; required information always uses highly readable print typography.
- Notebook ruling, margins, tabs, stamps, labels, and layered paper remain subordinate to teaching content. Decorative objects never imitate controls; real controls may take a physical-object form only when their visible label, hierarchy, and operation stay obvious.
- Binding holes, paper grain, tape, and stacked-sheet effects are CSS-only surface cues; keep them subtle, non-interactive, and outside the reading flow.
- The application is one interactive workbook world: wide screens may reveal the surrounding desk and open-page depth, while narrow screens become a simple pocket notebook.
- Game-like visual craft means responsive physical feedback and authored page scenes, never points, rewards, locked content, or learning delays.
