# Learner-facing authoring examples

Use these examples when writing or auditing lessons, prompts, answers, and feedback. Read the canonical [lesson responsibilities](../../../../specs/content-authoring.md#lesson-section-responsibilities), [vocabulary policy](../../../../specs/content-authoring.md#vocabulary-ownership-and-variety), [answer policy](../../../../specs/content-authoring.md#exercise-definitions-and-accepted-answers), and [prompt style](../../../../specs/content-authoring.md#prompt-style-across-packs) first. The examples illustrate individual corrections; apply the full policy to the finished item.

## Inventory a broad correction

Search all installed lessons, scored exercises, and optional practice in the affected class. Group findings by issue type, record counts and intentional exceptions, and show one representative before/after example per category before bulk wording edits unless the user waived that preview. Use the complete inventory for the correction and final audit.

## Replace internal shorthand

Before:

> These endings are supplied, so they are not new decisions here.

After, when the endings are actually displayed:

> The endings are already provided. You only need to choose the correct consonant form.

This puts the learner's action first. Follow the policy when a supporting form has yet to be supplied.

## Demonstrate a verb in use

For a lesson demonstrating verb usage, replace:

> nukkua → minä nukun

with:

> Minä nukun hyvin. — I sleep well.

An isolated form can still serve a task whose explicit target is that morphology.

## Show the intended sentence

Before:

> ____ ovat kotona. (kirja)

After:

> Complete “The books are at home.” ____ ovat kotona. Use kirja (“book”).

Similarly, replace “Build the Finnish sentence” with “Build ‘The cats are at home.’” and the required task controls. Inventory supporting forms separately; showing the meaning does not settle vocabulary or grammar scope.

## Resolve second-person meaning

Before:

> Write “You are at home.” in Finnish.

For one person:

> Address one person: write “You are at home.” in Finnish.

For polite singular:

> Address one person politely with te: write “You are at home.” in Finnish.

Use the policy's corresponding plural cue for a group. Compare the cue with the actual expected form before approval.

## Make supplied-stem claims accurate

Before:

> Use the supplied stem luke-.

when only `lukea` is visible.

When stem formation is supporting context, show `luke-` in the prompt before the learner answers. Explaining it only after submission does not supply it.

When forming that stem is the explicit assessed target, teach the derivation, ask the learner to form it, and explain the derivation in feedback. Describe it as a derived stem; calling it “supplied” requires its pre-response display. See the policy's form-transformation rule.

## Translate reference naturally

Before:

> `Onko se kirja kotona?` → “Is the previously identified book at home?”

After:

> “Is the book at home?”

Accept “Is that book at home?” when visible context supports it. Put a prior-mention cue outside the quoted target if choosing Finnish `se` is the assessed contrast. Review all accepted English frames under the answer policy.

## Show vocabulary ownership

When a worked example uses `He ovat koulussa.`, a lesson that introduced only its new words may omit needed context. Show `koulussa — at school` under **Used again** when its introduction belongs to the declared prerequisite chain. Otherwise, visibly translated context can belong under **Supplied in examples**. Verify the actual Finnish, English, and type against its introduction.

For a possession pack whose grammar bases include `minä`, teach `minulla` in the grammar explanation or possessor table. Supporting `pallo` and supplied `palloa` remain vocabulary when they are outside that pack's grammar-only scope.

## Inspect the rendered result

Apply the complete [final assessment](../../../../specs/content-authoring.md#5-final-finnish-teaching-pedagogy-assessment) to the inventoried class. Read prompts before submission and feedback after it; compare lesson vocabulary and exercise recall declarations with their actual Finnish and meanings. Check natural alternatives with grading, and test both a predictable error and an unmatched answer to inspect specific and general feedback.

Record every audited item, exception, inflection judgment, correction, and limitation. Preserve compatible metadata and history under the policy's [version guidance](../../../../specs/content-authoring.md#versions-and-learner-history).
